### 4. Dereferenceable Sources (Chronological Bibliography)

* **65 AD — Seneca, Lucius Annaeus. *Epistulae Morales ad Lucilium*** (
Frames manual note-taking as gathering fragments across diverse sources and physically transcribing them to fuse external observations into coherent, personal judgment.
* **95 AD — Quintilian (Marcus Fabius Quintilianus). *Institutio Oratoria*, Book XI.**
Documents the classical rhetorical observation that the physical act of handwriting—combining tactile resistance, deliberate pacing, and spatial layout on the page—serves directly as a mnemonic mechanism.
* **170 AD — Aurelius, Marcus. *Ta eis heauton* (*Meditations*).**
Exemplifies the unindexed chronological notebook written strictly as an immediate cognitive instrument for an audience of one, prior to any formal indexing scheme.
* **1597 — Bacon, Francis. *"Of Studies"* (*Essays*).**
Distills the functional utility of writing into three dry propositions—that it enforces exactness of thought, anchors memory, and eliminates the need to carry raw trivia in working recall.
* **1685 — Locke, John. *"Méthode nouvelle de dresser des recueils"* (*A New Method of a Common-Place-Book*). *Bibliothèque Universelle et Historique*.**
Documents the transition from unindexed chronological notebooks to a minimal, vowel-and-consonant retrieval grid—demonstrating that indexing architecture should only be introduced after physical volume demands it, while manual transcription itself acts as an effective, if unglamorous, *shite filter*.
* **1748 — Hume, David. *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*. A. Millar.**
Supplies the epistemological triage criterion ("Hume's Fork"): separating verifiable *matters of fact* and *relations of ideas* from ungrounded speculation—the philosophical precursor to a high-friction personal information filter.
* **1957 — Popper, Karl. *The Poverty of Historicism*. Routledge & Kegan Paul.**
Refutes deterministic, totalizing planning by proving that future knowledge cannot be rationally forecasted—justifying piecemeal, low-overhead workflows over premature information architectures.
* **1967 — Debord, Guy. *La Société du spectacle* (*The Society of the Spectacle*). Buchet-Chastel.**
Provides the theoretical grounding for *détournement*—the practice of hijacking commercial or ideological artifacts with minor, subversive alterations to neutralize their intended psychological hook, as observed on the Leith bus shelter.
* **1995 — de Rosnay, Joël. *L'Homme symbiotique* (*The Symbiotic Man*). Éditions du Seuil.**
Introduces the concept of *infobésité* (infobesity) at the dawn of the commercial web, framing information overload as an evolutionary mismatch between Paleolithic foraging instincts and frictionless digital abundance.
