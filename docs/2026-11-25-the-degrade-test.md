# The degrade test — output is not capability

## The claim and the inference

Two sentences, and only one of them is true.

**True:** *AI increased my craft skills.* The notebook cover exists, the flap
solves a real problem, and the way of working changed.

**Not true, and not an inference from it:** *therefore put it in front of
children.* The first sentence is a statement about a competent maker. The second
is the same statement with its boundary conditions removed — which is **frame
defaulting** in the pedagogic register: the default frame is *the operator*, and
the population being reasoned about has departed from it in the one parameter that
matters.

## What the flap proves

A model cannot tell you where your third finger rests. The pinkie-and-ring grip is
knowledge that comes from *carrying the thing* — from a notebook slipping, from a
hand that has already learnt its own geometry.

So the anecdote contains its own proof that the assistant was not the source of
the skill. **The hand was there first.**

## The mechanism, named

What the assistant actually did is **demand-side**. It did not supply capability;
it changed how much capability was worth spending.

- **Motive.** A notation problem — naming documents so they can be referenced
  across silos — produced a *demand* for a physical object that carries names.
- **Attention.** Absorbing the clerical and articulatory load is most of what a
  model does. Attention is fungible, and what it was spent on instead was a hand
  and a sheet of card.
- **Interlocution.** A half-formed idea can be said aloud at 11pm. That raises the
  *number of things attempted*, and attempting is upstream of skill without being
  skill.

All three are demand. None of them are supply. **The assistant can make you
attempt things that require skill; it is your prior skill that cashes the
attempt.**

## Where the nursery inference inverts

| | the competent maker | a child |
|---|---|---|
| prior hand-knowledge | present — the flap is evidence | absent |
| attention freed | redirected into making | redirected into what? |
| what the model supplies | a spur | an **artefact** |
| failure | theirs, and legible | absorbed before it is felt |

A child's binding constraint is not attention to craft. It is the thousand small
failures that *are* haptic competence. Substituting a model's output for those
failures substitutes the answer for the attempt — the same error as handing over a
finished proof. And the failure is invisible from outside: you cannot tell whether
the child learnt to cut card or learnt to ask.

*Conflation, and then confusion* — in that order. The confusion is downstream.

## The test

> **Remove the dependency. Can the thing still be made?**

If yes, what was gained is a **capability**. If no, what increased was an
**output** — and it will not survive the first time the tool is taken away.

This is the same test this silo applies to its own artefacts: the export runs the
degrade with `pandoc` absent and compares bytes; the ladder's third stage exists
because the fallback had to be exercised, not assumed; `ADR-MATH-GRAMMAR-076`
chose *shown exactly as written* over a rendering that only holds when an engine
is installed. **A claim that something works carries a statement about what
happens without it, or it is a claim about a demo.**

Applied to a person, the test is cheap, and it is the only one that distinguishes
skill from transaction.

## The version that survives

Three forms of the pedagogy argument are not the fallacy:

- **Interlocutor, not fabricator.** The model asks questions *around* the attempt;
  it does not produce the object being judged. The child still holds the card and
  still cuts it wrong.
- **The adult's attention.** The same mechanism as the anecdote — absorb the
  clerical load so the scarce resource, an adult's hand-time with a child, is
  spent on hands. This one is genuinely demand-side and is where the leverage is.
- **Failure stays legible to the child.** The admissibility gate for every other
  version. If the child cannot tell what went wrong, nothing was learnt and they
  were merely served.

## Out of scope

This is a test for one claim, not an argument about education policy, and not a
verdict on anyone's classroom. It says what would have to be true for *"AI
increased my craft skills"* to transfer to someone without the craft, and what to
measure if you want to know whether it did.

## Prior art

- **Frame defaulting** (`docs/2026-10-08-frame-defaulting.md`) — the same law in
  the physics register: the prior is the mean over the training frame, applied
  regardless of the boundary conditions. Here the frame is a competent adult.
- The **degrade ladder** and the byte-identical degrade check
  (`decisions/096-the-export-identifies-more-maths.md`) — the test, applied to an
  artefact rather than a person.
- **Predictably adequate** — a raised floor is a real gain, and it is the gain the
  demand-side reading describes: variance compressed, ceiling unchanged. Nothing
  here says the tool is worthless; it says the tool is not the teacher.
