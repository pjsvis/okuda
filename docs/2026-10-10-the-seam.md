# The seam — where the invariants stop, and how to cross

> **A silo's gates are exactly as wide as its boundary.** `just check` proves the
> silo and proves nothing about a file it wrote somewhere else. That is not a
> weakness of the gates; it is what a boundary *is*. The seam is where the
> invariants stop — and therefore where the trouble lives.

## What the silo buys: context, attribution, verification

The three things the operator named as scrambling when a crossing goes wrong are
exactly the three the silo provides for free *inside*:

| property | inside the silo | at a bad crossing |
|---|---|---|
| **context** | the bounded context: why this exists, what it refuses, what it agreed | the receiver sees a file with no history; the sender sees the effect with no feedback |
| **attribution** | one authority per fact; a derived artefact declares itself derived | whose file is it? who owns the commit? (a copy written through a symlink belongs cleanly to neither side) |
| **verification** | gates as wide as the boundary; every claim names its instrument | nothing checks a crossing — no gate spans the seam |

So the silo model **side-swerves a whole class** of problems: keep the boundary,
and context, attribution and verification hold inside it. The seam is the one
place all three leak at once, and the discipline is about crossing without the
leak.

## The rule — messages, not shared files

**Silos communicate by message. A crossing declares itself.**

This is the actor model, and it is not a metaphor here: an actor never mutates
another actor's state, it sends a message. Concretely:

- the **sender** owns the content and publishes it — a copy, a stamp, a message;
- the **receiver** owns its copy, its derived state (its registers, its
  manifests), and **the commit**;
- the sender **never reaches in**, and never does the receiver's commit.

An inbox like `msgs/from-*` is the mechanism: unregistered, so touching it moves
no derived state, and the receiver processes it on its own terms.

**Why not a symlink.** A symlink across a seam is *shared memory*: the receiver
depends on the sender's path and state, and neither side owns the file. A **copy
is a message** — the receiver owns it, may adapt it, and can evolve without the
sender. The defect is not that a copy can drift from its source; the defect is a
crossing that does not say so.

## Undeclared scope is the defect class

The mirror of the rule, and the day's most expensive lesson:

> **Undeclared scope is the defect class — not non-blocking scope.**

A gate that states its limit is not a false signal; a gate that does not is. The
worked instance: a register whose stored content hashes had drifted was reported
as a gap — and the gate's own header had already declared that check
*informational in v1*. The measurement was right and the verdict was wrong, which
is the same shape as the symlink: the pointer was never the problem, the
**silence** was.

## The faff is the instrument

The defect above is measurable in behaviour, and the measurement is where the
most time goes. The operator's sketch of a crossing gone wrong:

> *"what's going on? don't know! do you want me to find out? yes! ok, what do you
> want me to find out? I don't know, can't you tell me? let me look at some stuff
> and see if I can make sense of it! ok!…"*

That is not ignorance. Outside a silo **everyone is ignorant, and that is
unavoidable** — the boundary is what makes each silo knowable at all. It is
**unnamed** ignorance:

| named | unnamed |
|---|---|
| *there is a message in your inbox* | *what's going on? don't know!* |
| *SYSTEM.md is a copy of okuda's system.md* | *let me look at some stuff and see if I can make sense of it* |
| *that register's hashes are informational in v1* | *can't you tell me?* |

**Named, ignorance is work. Unnamed, it is rummaging.** Every named form above is
a sentence that could have been written into an artefact — the whole rule, felt
rather than argued.

**Why the length is the measure.** The faff is negotiation over an address space
that does not resolve: with no names, the parties must establish context by
*conversation* — the worst possible map, because it is ephemeral, unaddressable
and rebuilt from scratch every time. So **the length of the negotiation measures
how much of the interface is undeclared.** A crossing with a written interface
takes one message; a crossing without one takes an afternoon.

**The operational consequence.** *When the faff starts, stop and declare
something.* Do not open an investigation — write a sentence into an artefact.
The sketch above is the symptom, not the cure.

**And the economics.** The faff is paid **once, up front, as a declaration**,
after which the crossing is free. The first silo spin-off cost a morning of
contradictions, a rejected review and two rounds over a register; the second took
seconds and produced no faff at all, because the declaration
(`PLY-COMMISSION`) was already written. **A declared seam is faff spent once; an
undeclared one is faff spent every time.**

## Three instances, one shape

- **The two-hop symlink.** `~/.pi/agent/AGENTS.md` resolved into another repo's
  file; `cp` followed it silently and left the receiver dirty — attribution
  scrambled, verification absent, context lost. Fixed by *declaring*: resolve the
  chain, name the file, name the repo, name the uncommitted state
  (`scripts/protocol-install.sh`).
- **The register that was fine.** Measured hashes, missed the header. Reported as
  a defect; corrected by the receiver, whose gate had declared its own scope.
- **The inbox delivery.** A request crossed a seam the right way: a message in
  `msgs/from-mac/`; the receiver filed it in its own `docs/`, regenerated **its**
  register for that file only, strengthened the artefact with project precedent,
  and acked. Four commits, two repositories, **no shared mutable byte**.

## The test

For any crossing, can a stranger say — **from the artefacts alone** — what wrote
it, where it landed, and who must commit it? If not, the crossing is the defect,
whatever the pointer says.
