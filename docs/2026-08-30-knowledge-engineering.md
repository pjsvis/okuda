### The Cognitive Scaling Heuristic

```
[ Human Working Memory ] ──► ~7±2 items (The Vector Table: ≤10 targets)
[ Cognitive Session    ] ──► 1 Chapter / 1 Brief (1,500 – 3,500 words)
[ Complete Domain      ] ──► 1 Volume / 1 Silo (~50 – 100 Vectors)

```

* **The Book as the Upper Bounded Context:** A standard non-fiction book runs 200–300 pages across 10–15 chapters. Humanity didn't settle on this format by accident; it represents the maximum semantic volume a human mind can synthesize before cognitive fatigue forces a consolidation pass.
* **The Document as the Atomic Chapter:** A document (brief, ADR, playbook) is deliberately smaller—typically 1 to 5 pages. It is a single, self-contained unit of intent designed to be loaded, verified, and actuated in a single cognitive sitting.
* **Miller's Law at the Control Surface ($\le 10$):** Constraining our dynamic Vector Tables to a single digit or a tight two-digit spread (under 10 primary vectors per viewport) mirrors how working memory actually operates. The moment you give someone an un-filtered list of 80 options, paralysis sets in. If an index has 70 items, it is not an index—it is an un-factored swamp.

### Why Modern "Knowledge Graphs" Failed

Modern enterprise software lost its mind by abandoning these physical scaling factors:

* **The Infinite Canvas Trap:** Replacing chapters and pages with infinite, zoomable 2D whiteboards where thousands of micro-nodes float in space. It looks impressive in marketing videos, but it produces instant spatial disorientation.
* **The Deep Hierarchy Lie:** Hiding atomic thoughts inside 15-level folder trees where no human can hold the contextual path in their head.
* **The Firehose Vector Dump:** Presenting 200 search results instead of an authoritative, bounded selection of top-priority vectors.

### The Operational Heuristic

If a knowledge corpus respects the ergonomic scale of a book—subdividing into crisp, atomic documents bounded by $\le 10$ choices at the retrieval surface—it remains permanently intelligible to both a human operator and an AI agent.

It keeps the entire knowledge base scaled to the human mind: **atomic pages, clear chapter boundaries, tight vector tables, zero infinite-scroll soup.**

### The Evolutionary Trajectory

```
[Prompt Engineering] ──► [Context Engineering] ──► [Harness Engineering] ──► [Knowledge Engineering]
   "Tricking the model"     "Stuffing the window"    "Scaffolding the loops"    "Factoring the ground truth"

```

* **Prompt Engineering (The Magic Incantation Phase):** Obsessing over token phrasing, *"act as an expert,"* and emotional pleading. A necessary first step, but fundamentally brittle and artisanal.
* **Context Engineering (The Packing Problem Phase):** Managing token budgets, RAG chunks, dynamic retrieval, and sliding windows. Useful mechanics, but prone to garbage-in/garbage-out retrieval swamps.
* **Harness Engineering (The Scaffolding Phase):** Tool-calling wrappers, agentic loops, sub-agent delegations, and eval pipelines. Critical systems engineering, but empty without bounded domain truth.
* **Knowledge Engineering (The Sovereign Ground Truth):** The synthesis of all three. Structuring the underlying domain corpus into bounded, deterministic, multi-angled artifacts (briefs, ADRs, ISO timestamps, flat silos, and tight **Vector Tables**) so that the computer—human or machine—can actuate intent without ambiguity.

### Respecting the Antecedents

This doesn't disparage the 1980s expert-systems era of Knowledge Engineering, nor does it dismiss the modern LLM toolchain. It integrates them:

* **The Classical Antecedent:** The 1980s got the intent right—formalizing domain ontologies, rules, and decision graphs—but the tooling (brittle LISP inference engines and manual propositional logic) couldn't handle real-world entropy.
* **The Modern Substrate:** Modern LLMs provide the flexible, robust semantic inference engine that classical Knowledge Engineering always dreamed of.
* **The New Discipline:** Knowledge Engineering in the Okuda/TFS paradigm is about building the **over-complete, low-entropy document corpus**:
1. Bounded scaling factors (atomic documents, $\le 10$ choices per Vector Table).
2. Multi-modal verification (spatial terminal matrices and auditory cadence linters).
3. Absolute operational boundaries (TFS Exclusion, the Derrida Veto, flat directory silos).



When the knowledge is engineered with physical rigor, you don't need desperate prompt hacks or convoluted agent swarms. The machine reads the vector, executes the Janeway command, and the intent actuates into reality.
