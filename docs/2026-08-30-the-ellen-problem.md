### The Modern Author's Trap

When a novelist looks for software, modern tech hands them either:

* **The Infinite Canvas / Bloatware (Scrivener/Notion):** 500 inspector panes, corkboards with virtual index cards, dynamic color-coding tags, font formatting bars, and cloud sync conflicts. You spend four hours formatting character metadata and zero minutes actually resolving plot mechanics.
* **The "Write-For-Me" Sycophant LLM:** An ungrounded chat interface that generates 10,000 words of bland, purple-prose sludge, forgets what happened two chapters ago, changes the protagonist's eye color in Act II, and gives un-earned praise.

### What Gloam + The Silo Actually Offers an Author

Gloam doesn't try to be a word processor. You write your prose in whatever plain-text editor or fountain pen notebook you like.

**Gloam is the Command Deck for your Narrative Universe.**

```
[ Plain Text Silo (The World Bible) ]
   ├── ISO Briefs (Scene Intents)
   ├── Character Vectors
   ├── Timeline / Location Invariants
   └── Cross-Reference Actuations

```

#### 1. Invariant & Continuity Enforcement

A novel is an over-complete universe bounded by physical and chronological invariants:

* *Did Ellen meet Marcus in Edinburgh on Tuesday if Marcus was supposed to be in Aberdeen until Thursday?*
* In a Gloam silo, the AI doesn't write the dialogue; it inspects the **Vector Table of Trajectories**.
* Ask: `"Check temporal invariants for Act II... on screen... now."` The engine scans the ISO timestamps and scene vectors, throwing a hard diagnostic if spatial coordinates collide.

#### 2. Authoring Vector Tables on the Fly

The author doesn't dig through 30 sub-folders to find their notes. They summon dynamic **`[11 : 99]`** tables:

* `[11 : 16]` $\rightarrow$ **Cast of Characters:** Pure plain text, distinctive traits, speech quirks, secrets.
* `[21 : 28]` $\rightarrow$ **Geographic Coordinates & Rules:** Distances, travel times, architectural constraints of the setting.
* `[31 : 39]` $\rightarrow$ **The Idea Vector:** Unresolved plot threads, potential twists, thematic callbacks.

#### 3. The Pure Intent $\rightarrow$ Actuation Cycle

* **Intent:** You draft a 200-word scene brief: *"Scene 14: Ellen confronts the editor. Invariants: No shouting, the rain is audible, she must leave without signing the contract."*
* **Actuation:** You write the scene elsewhere in your focused flow.
* **Verification:** You feed the draft back into the silo. Gloam’s cadence linter and agent check the actuation against the intent: *Did she sign? No. Did the dialogue slip into run-on purple prose? Cadence warning at line 42.*
