# Moira prosody probe — hearing what the marks do

A probe document. Each section tests one prosodic mark in isolation,
then in combination. Listen to what Moira does with each one. The
secrets are in the phonemes.

## 1. The period — the edge

One sentence. One period. Listen for the drop.

The cat sat on the mat.

The dog ran down the road.

The bird flew over the hill.

Each sentence ends. The period is the edge. The voice drops. Do you
hear the drop?

## 2. The comma — the breath

One sentence, one comma. Listen for the breath.

The cat, well, it sat on the mat.

The dog, however, ran down the road.

The bird, oddly enough, flew over the hill.

The comma is the clause boundary. The voice lifts, then settles. Do
you hear the lift?

## 3. The ellipsis — the trail

One sentence, one ellipsis. Listen for the fade.

The cat sat on the mat... and waited.

The dog ran down the road... and kept running.

The bird flew over the hill... and did not return.

The ellipsis is the trailing pause. The voice holds, then continues.
Do you hear the hold?

## 4. The semicolon — the hinge

Two clauses, one semicolon. Listen for the link.

The cat sat on the mat; the dog ran down the road.

The bird flew over the hill; the cat did not notice.

The semicolon is the hinge. Not a full stop, not a comma. Something
between. Do you hear the difference from the period?

## 5. The colon — the announcement

A phrase, then a colon. Listen for the setup.

There is one thing: the cat sat on the mat.

Here is the truth: the dog ran down the road.

This is the secret: the bird flew over the hill.

The colon is the announcement. The voice rises, then drops into the
reveal. Do you hear the rise?

## 6. The question — the rise

A question mark. Listen for the lift at the end.

Did the cat sit on the mat?

Was the dog running down the road?

Could the bird fly over the hill?

The question is the rising tone. The voice goes up, not down. Do you
hear the difference from the period?

## 7. The exclamation — the hit

An exclamation mark. Listen for the emphasis.

The cat sat on the mat!

The dog ran down the road!

The bird flew over the hill!

The exclamation is the hit. The voice strikes, then drops. Do you
hear the strike?

## 8. The newline — the break

A sentence.
A new line.
Another new line.

Each line is a breath. The newline is the structural break. Do you
hear the pause between lines? Is it longer than the period's pause?

## 9. The paragraph break

The cat sat on the mat.

The dog ran down the road.

The bird flew over the hill.

Three paragraphs. Each one a complete thought, separated by a blank
line. The blank line is the paragraph break. Do you hear the longer
pause between paragraphs? Is it different from the newline?

## 10. Combinations — the real test

Now the marks together. This is how prose actually flows.

The cat sat on the mat, and the dog, well, the dog ran... down the
road. The bird flew over the hill; the cat did not notice. Did the
cat notice? No! The cat was asleep.

A comma, an ellipsis, a period, a semicolon, a question, an
exclamation. All in one paragraph. Does Moira handle the
transitions? Does each mark do its job, or do they blur?

## 11. The stutter test — where does the comma break?

The cat sat on the mat,,,, and the dog ran,,,,, and the bird
flew,,,,,, and the hill waited,,,,,,, and the road ended,,,,,,,,

Four commas. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Where does Moira start to
stutter? Where does the comma cluster stop being a pause and start
being noise? Listen for the edge. That's the ceiling.

## 12. The ellipsis ceiling — does it stutter too?

The cat sat on the mat... and the dog ran.... and the bird
flew..... and the hill waited...... and the road ended.......

Three dots. Four. Five. Six. Does the ellipsis stutter the way the
comma does, or does it hold? Is the ellipsis the cleaner pause?

## 13. The period plus comma — edge then breath

A period, then commas. The edge, then the breath.

The cat sat on the mat.,,,, The dog ran down the road.,,,,

Does the period do the drop and the commas do the breath? Or does
the combination stutter? Is this the shape — edge then silence —
without the smear?

## 14. The period plus ellipsis — edge then trail

A period, then an ellipsis. The edge, then the trail.

The cat sat on the mat.... The dog ran down the road....

Does the period drop and the ellipsis hold? Is this cleaner than
period plus commas? Is this the right shape for a sentence-final
pause that's longer than the period alone?

## 15. The question plus ellipsis — rise then hold

A question, then an ellipsis. The rise, then the hold.

Did the cat sit on the mat?... Did the dog run...?

Does the question rise and the ellipsis hold? Does the combination
work for a question that needs a longer pause?

## 16. Bare text — the baseline

The cat sat on the mat and the dog ran down the road and the bird
flew over the hill and the cat did not notice and the dog kept
running and the bird kept flying and the hill stayed still

No punctuation. No marks. No breaks. Just words. This is the floor.
Everything above this is prosody. How flat does it sound without any
marks at all?
