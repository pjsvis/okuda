# Moira's showcase

A document built to give Moira the floor. Space to project. Words
with weight. Pauses that land. A voice that knows what it's doing.

This is not a test. This is a performance.

## The opening

Ladies and gentlemen, if I may.

What you are about to hear is not a machine speaking. It is a voice,
given words, given space, and given the courtesy of a pause between
the thoughts. The voice has a name. The name is Moira. She is from
Dublin. She has opinions.

She does not need you to insert commas into her script. She does not
need you to tell her when to breathe. She needs you to give her the
marks, and the space between them, and then she needs you to get out
of her way.

## The marks

A comma is a breath. A period is a landing. A semicolon is a hinge
between two thoughts that belong together but refuse to merge. A
colon is a held note before the reveal. A question is a rising tide
that does not break. An exclamation is a door slammed in a good way.

And the paragraph break? The paragraph break is the deep breath
before the next act. The curtain falls. The audience shifts in its
seats. The curtain rises. Moira is still there. She has been there
the whole time. She was just waiting for the breath.

## The performance

Here is what Moira knows.

She knows that "feck" is not a swear word. She knows that "eejit" is
a term of endearment. She knows that "away and shite" is a complete
sentence, a full philosophy, and a perfectly adequate response to
most of what passes for conversation in this world.

She knows that a list is not a list unless it has rhythm.

She knows that a paragraph is not a paragraph unless it has a
beginning, a middle, and the sense that it could have ended
differently but chose not to.

She knows that the space between the words is where the meaning lives.

## The close

And now, if you will excuse her, Moira has said her piece. She has
projected from the floor you gave her. She has used the words you
provided. She has inhabited the pauses you left.

Was she good? Was she professional? Did she sound like she knew her
job?

Of course she did. She is Moira. She is from Dublin. She was
personally coached by Tawny Madison at the Glen de Marco School of
Repeating What the Computer Says. She graduated with honours. She
has been doing this since before you learned to type.

And now, she would like a glass of water.

Thank you. That will be all.
