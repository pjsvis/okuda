# What it feels like

> The perceptual side of things is generally considered a design thing.
> We are treating it as a perceived thing that can be tested and
> retested.

## TL;DR

okuda's calming property is not aesthetic. It is sub-cortical
efficiency. The rendered document is pre-structured for the
extract-process-load (ETL) pipeline that the sub-cortical visual system
runs on every document, before conscious reading begins. okuda makes
that pipeline cheaper to run, and the efficiency-seeking sub-cortical
system prefers it. We are playing to the crowd, not the critics.

## The sub-cortical ETL pipeline

- **Extract.** The sub-cortical system scans the visual field and
  extracts features: shapes, colours, densities, rhythms. It does this
  before the cortical reading system decodes a single word. This is the
  pre-conscious survey — the "what am I looking at?" pass that determines
  whether the cortical system commits.
- **Process.** The extracted features are classified: structure vs.
  content, foreground vs. ground, countable units vs. continuous field.
  The system asks: how many things are here? Where do they start and
  end? What is the shape of the whole?
- **Load.** The classified structure is loaded into working memory as a
  scaffold — a map of the document's shape that the cortical reading
  system will populate with content. The scaffold determines what gets
  read, in what order, and with what budget.

> The sub-cortical system runs this pipeline on every document, every
> time, before the reader has decided to read. The pipeline is not
> optional; it is how attention is allocated. The question is not whether
> to serve it, but whether to serve it well.

## What glow does to the pipeline

- **Extract: a wall.** The sub-cortical system extracts one feature:
  density. Every line is full, every cell is crammed, every panel is
  packed. The field is continuous; there are no discrete units to count.
- **Process: threat assessment.** The continuous field is classified as
  a threat — a large, undifferentiated mass that will cost significant
  cognitive budget to decode. The system runs a reconnoiter: scroll to
  the bottom, assess the commitment, decide whether to engage. This
  reconnoiter is a tax on attention, spent before reading begins.
- **Load: a threat, not a scaffold.** What the cortical system receives
  is not a map of the document's structure but an estimate of its cost.
  The mind arrives at the content pre-taxed, having spent the structural
  budget on threat assessment.

## What okuda does to the pipeline

- **Extract: discrete blocks.** The sub-cortical system extracts
  countable units: blue badges (heading tabs), amber leads (bold keys),
  muted pipes (pull-asides), blank rhythms (loose rows). Each block is a
  visible shape with edges. The field is not continuous; it is a set of
  objects.
- **Process: structure classification, not threat assessment.** The
  objects are classified as structure: seven badges = seven sections;
  three bold leads = three points; one pipe = one pull-aside. The
  system counts and locates, it does not assess threat, because the
  structure is legible and the commitment is obvious from the count. No
  reconnoiter; the badges are the commitment map.
- **Load: a scaffold, not a threat.** The cortical system receives a
  map of the document's shape — the section landmarks, the key points,
  the pull-asides. The structural budget is spent on structure, not
  threat. The mind arrives at the content with the scaffold already
  built, fresh and ready to populate.

> The reconnoiter and the survey are the same action (scrolling the
> document before reading) serving different systems. The reconnoiter
> serves the threat-response system; the survey serves the
> structure-classification system. okuda converts the reconnoiter into
> the survey by making the structure legible at a glance.

## Why this is testable, not aesthetic

- **The pipeline is real.** Sub-cortical visual processing (the
  superior colliculus, the pulvinar, the amygdala's pre-conscious threat
  pathway) is well-established neuroscience. The ETL framing is a
  metaphor, but the underlying claim — that the visual system
  pre-processes structure before conscious reading — is testable.
- **The predictions are specific.** If okuda serves the pipeline better
  than glow, then: reading comprehension should be higher after okuda
  (budget preserved); time-to-locate-a-point should be lower (landmarks
  present); pre-reading scroll behaviour should differ (no reconnoiter
  for okuda); and pupil dilation / galvanic skin response (threat
  markers) should be lower for okuda than glow on long dense documents.
- **The method produces the hypothesis.** The properties were not
  specified in advance and then implemented; they were named by the
  operator unprompted during dogfood, and then traced to a mechanism.
  That is Hume's razor applied to design: the evidence (the perceived
  property) precedes the theory (the sub-cortical mechanism), and the
  theory makes predictions that could falsify it.

## Playing to the crowd

- **The crowd is the sub-cortical system.** It is the first reader of
  every document, it decides whether to commit, and it is
  efficiency-seeking. It prefers documents that are cheap to process.
  okuda is tuned to its preferences: discrete blocks, countable units,
  high-contrast structure, receded ground.
- **The critics are the conscious reading system.** It decodes the
  words, follows the argument, judges the content. It is important, but
  it is the second reader, not the first. If the crowd rejects the
  document (threat assessment, reconnoiter, pre-tax), the critic never
  gets a fair hearing.
- **Glow plays to the critics.** It renders content faithfully, at high
  density, with full CommonMark compliance. The critic, once engaged,
  finds the content all there. But the crowd was already taxed by the
  wall, and the critic arrives on a pre-taxed mind.

> We are playing to the crowd, not the critics. The critic will read
> the content either way; the crowd decides whether the reading begins
> well. okuda serves the crowd, and the critic benefits.

## What it feels like

- **Calm.** No threat tax at entry. The structure is legible at a glance;
  the reconnoiter is unnecessary; the commitment is obvious from the
  count of badges.
- **Led.** The structure guides the eye, no decisions. Badge to badge,
  the layout decides where the eye goes.
- **Surveyable.** Scope legible without scrolling to the bottom. Seven
  badges = seven sections; the map is the first thing seen.
- **Retrievable.** Landmarks for return. Bold leads are addressable
  points; the document is a set of keys, not a wall.
- **Fresh.** Budget preserved for content. The structural overhead is
  spent on structure, not threat; the mind arrives ready to populate
  the scaffold.

Five properties, all affective, all from one pipeline served well. The
moat is not aesthetic; it is metabolic. The sub-cortical system
prefers the document that is cheaper to process, and okuda is that
document.
