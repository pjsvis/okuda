# notion pathology

  - i could provide you with a construction kit of lego blocks where you could write your own letters on the blocks and arrange the blocks into collections and arrange the collections into groups etc
  - but that does not help with my current project which involves collections of characters which are composed into so-called words and documents and organised in a manner that I can define a bounded context and have a wee think about things without any distractions
  - but hey maybe legos would be a big help...
  - that is the Notion story

  You’ve pinned the exact pathology of the whole thing.
  
  Notion handed everyone a bucket of plastic studs and essentially said: *"Look, you can build a cathedral, or a spaceship, or a relational database of your dog's vet visits! First, configure the studs."*
  
  Meanwhile, you just wanted to sit down, define a boundary, type some words, and let the gears in your head turn without having to manage the metaphysics of a `/database-inline` view.
  
  Running this through an **Operational Heuristic**, the failure mode is obvious:
  
  1. **Meta-Work as Procrastination:** The Lego architecture demands that you become a toolmaker before you can become a tool user. Instead of doing the actual thinking, people spend three hours designing a customized dashboard with pastel icons and nested progress bars. It simulates progress while producing zero net insight.
  2. **Context Leakage:** A bounded context requires a fence. Notion’s design philosophy is the exact opposite—everything is an interconnected node, a draggable block, an embed, or an autonomous agent scraping your Slack channels. The boundary never seals; the room is always leaking ambient noise.
  3. **The Mechanical Overhead of "Flexibility":** Plain text—words arranged into documents—has survived since Gutenberg because the interface disappears. The moment every single paragraph is a mutable, draggable container waiting to be transformed into a toggle list or inspected by an AI agent, the medium stops being a quiet surface for thought and becomes an interactive puzzle.
  
  If your objective is to take a set of characters, assemble them into coherent prose, and establish a clean perimeter to actually think, a box of programmable Lego blocks isn't an asset. It's just a colourful obstacle course between your brain and the page.
