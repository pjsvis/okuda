# No file tree, no document outline — the viewer is content, not menu

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Type:** design note (the shite-filter posture applied to the viewer itself; third leg of the stance)
**Status:** The answer to "show the file structure in the viewer" and "show an outline of the document." The answer is foxtrot oscar, and the reason generalises.

---

## The answer

Foxtrot oscar. To those who say we should show the file structure of the repo in the viewer, or an outline of the structure of the document, the answer is the same the silo gives to all scaffolding: foxtrot oscar (see `docs/2026-08-29-tfs-exclusion-principle.md`). The reason is that both are menus, and the silo does content over menu (`docs/2026-09-03-content-over-menu.md`). The file tree is the menu of the silo; the outline is the menu of the document. Both are shite getting in the way of the content.

## The two rejections, separate sins

**The file tree is the wrong scale.** A list of files tells you nothing. The files and directories are names of things you don't know about, and quite possibly do not want to know about. A directory called `decisions/` tells you nothing about why any decision matters; a root doc titled `2026-09-05-i811-dual-layer-output.md` (date-prefixed, the silo's own convention) tells you *when* someone fought and *what* they fought about. The tree is a directory listing; the date-prefixed root docs are *clickbait that earned its headline*. Showing the tree instead of the docs is showing the container instead of the content — the menu instead of the meal, exactly the ZOG failure the silo exists to refuse. The tree forces the silo's ontology (what's a brief, what's a decision, what's a tier) onto the reader before they've decided the silo is worth their time. The shelf's whole job is to make the case *without* requiring that ontology.

**The outline is the wrong form.** The outline at least points at *this* document rather than the whole repo — so it's the subtler rejection. But it's still a menu: a menu of the document's skeleton, when the document itself is right there. The frame is the document-level shite filter (`docs/2026-09-05-frame-as-shite-filter.md`); the outline is the document-level menu. The frame shows the inspected minimum of the content; the outline shows the skeleton of the content. One is content; the other is chrome about content. The silo's posture is content over menu at every scale, so the outline goes the way of the tree.

## The objective, said in the deadpan

Our whole objective is to show a piece of clickbait and have you punch some keys. The root docs are the clickbait; the keys are the action; which direction you go with your keypresses is up to you. The silo *is* clickbait — and the difference between the silo's clickbait and the web's is that the silo's *delivers*. The web's clickbait is a lie ("you won't believe..."); the silo's is a *case* ("here is what is here, and why you'd bother"). Both want you to punch keys; only one deserves it. Naming the objective as clickbait is the deadpan at full opacity — it says the true thing (we want your keystrokes) in a voice that admits the manipulation and therefore can't be the i811 pattern's virtue-tinged manipulation. Say the unsayable, and the halo can't attach.

The silo doesn't funnel toward a goal (engagement, retention, conversion); it offers the grammar and lets the reader spend it on whatever they came for. A file tree *funnels* (here are the categories, pick one); a root doc *invites* (here's the case, go where you like). The tree is a funnel because a menu is always a funnel; the doc is a door because content is always a door.

## The stance, completed

This is the third leg of the shite filter. The search filters the corpus so you see the not-shite; the frame filters the document so you see the inspected minimum; the viewer refuses the tree and the outline so you see the content. Three filters, one posture: the reader's glass is for content; the shite (menus, trees, skeletons, dials) is what gets cut. The silo's whole job is to keep the glass clear — to allow you to see as much as you can without any shite getting in the way.

| Scale | Filter | What it cuts | What it keeps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corpus | search (shelf/dive) | the un-inspected corpus | root files; typed results |
| Document | frame (header + what fits) | the dangly tail | the inspected minimum |
| Viewer | content, not tree/outline | menus, trees, skeletons | the content itself |

## The one line

No tree, no outline, no dial — the viewer is content; the shite is what gets cut; the clickbait is the case; the keys are the direction.
