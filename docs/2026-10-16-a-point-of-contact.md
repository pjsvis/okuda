# A point of contact — the shop that sold books

> ◆ Two parties with no common language coordinate through objects they can both
> point at, and it holds. That is the thesis of
> `docs/2026-10-06-the-shared-artifact.md` — here it is, told as an afternoon in
> Tarifa, with no software in it at all.

## The story, as told

In Tarifa, Spain, the principal frequented a small local shop for wine and fish.
They had little Spanish; the owners had little English. They bonded over
**making change** — often the proprietor would point out a more efficient
change-making heuristic while they were transacting.

Once the principal returned a bottle of dodgy Málaga wine. For the next month
they were **required to taste every bottle before being allowed to leave the
shop**.

At the time they were reading a book by José Saramago, and noticed a point of
contact: the **long paragraphs**. So the next visit, they pointed out the long
paragraphs in the book. The owner went *aha!*, reached under the counter, and
produced a copy of Saramago's autobiography. Then he pointed at the ice-cream
freezer — which contained books rather than ice cream — as if to say *plenty
more in there*.

## What the agent added

Four readings, each one a shape the silo already has a word for.

**1. The index was typographic.** The principal identified the writer by a
*formal feature* — the long paragraph — before the name was ever available to
either party. That is recognition by structure, and it is the same move the silo
makes with `§NN` and with callsigns: the form is the handle, and the name is what
you look up afterwards. Two people with no shared language had a shared index
anyway — and it was made of shape, not words.

**2. The heuristic was the house's, and it was taught by doing.** The proprietor
corrected the arithmetic *during the transaction*, not by explaining. That is
`AGENTS.md`'s own disposition — *where it overlaps an external rule, the house
derived it better* — in a shop, in Spanish, over coins. And he bothered to do it
for a foreigner, because the foreigner's change is where the trust lives.

**3. The tasting month is a control, installed for one defect.** A single bad
bottle produced a gate that ran on **every** subsequent transaction. That is
*a detector needs a control* — except the detector was his palate and the cost of
the control was the principal's, before they were allowed to leave. Nobody would
have specified it. He did it because one defect had been shown, and the remedy he
chose was a **taste**, not a policy.

**4. The freezer is the good detail.** Wine and fish out front, where the
perishable stock lives; books round the back, under the counter and in the
ice-cream freezer, where the durable stock was kept cool. A shop whose real
inventory was a library, disclosed by a gesture rather than a catalogue —
*plenty more in there.*

## Why it is here

`docs/2026-10-06-the-shared-artifact.md` names five properties a document must
hold — **stable, addressable, inspectable, shared, purposive** — and argues that
two parties who can both *see, index and verify* the same object can think
together without a protocol between them.

The shop is that argument with the software removed. No shared tongue; a set of
objects both could point at — coins, a returned bottle, a paperback, a freezer
door. Each object carried what both parties needed, and neither had to trust the
other's telling. The change was **inspectable** (you can count it), the
paragraph was **addressable** (you can point at it), the autobiography was
**shared** (he had one, under the counter), and the freezer was **stable** (the
stock did not move while you looked at it). No API, no interface design — *the
objects were the interface*, which is the design principle this repo has been
circling all along.

And the shape recurs one layer down, in the row this session built: the same
economy of **subtraction** — show only what the filter and the gesture do not
already supply — and the same demand that a mark be **attributable**. A dim `§`,
a mid date, a bright line of content, a band: four objects on a line, each one
pointable, each one explicable from the page. *A point of contact* is what the
row is for.

## Boilerplate — what is and is not claimed

- The novel the principal was reading that week remains unnamed. The
  autobiography the owner produced is almost certainly **`Small Memories`**
  (Portuguese *As Pequenas Memórias*, 2006), the childhood memoir covering ages
  four to fifteen — named here as a probable identification, not a certainty.
- This is an **anecdote**, offered because it illustrates a principle the silo
  holds. It is not evidence for a claim, and it is not a customer story: no
  product appears in it.
- The parsing above is the agent's. The shopkeeper agreed that Saramago wrote
  long paragraphs. He did not agree to any of the four readings.
