# &stack — the document and its references

*The silo, the seam, the reader: one thesis, one page you can walk. `NN` dives,
`u` unwinds, `99` promotes a reference to a new main document.*

> **The world works by passing documents round.** A silo keeps one, a seam
> moves it between silos, and a reader walks from it into everything it cites.
> Three machines, one unit: **the document plus its references.**

## How to read this

This is a walkable document, not a deck. The body is the map — read it straight
through and you have the whole argument in four minutes. Each movement ends with
a reference to an **expansion**: a separate document holding the diagram and the
longer argument.

- **A `§NN` vector** is a painted jump, derived from the reference, never authored.
- **`NN`** actuates: the referenced document opens, rendered clean.
- **`u`** unwinds, back here.
- **`99`** promotes the reference to a fresh main document, if you want to walk on.

Nothing is hidden in a footnote. The overview is the map; the expansions are the
territory, and you visit exactly the ones you choose.

## One thesis, three machines

The document is the **unit of context**: it can be written, sent, kept, cited and
found, and it arrives with its relations already attached — its sender, its date,
its file, and above all its references. Three machines hold that unit.

- **The silo is the container.** A directory with a boundary is the unit of
  citation and the unit of context at once. Inside it, references resolve and
  claims can be checked.
- **The seam is the crossing.** A silo's gates are exactly as wide as its
  boundary, so the seam is where the invariants stop. Documents travel between
  silos as messages, not shared files.
- **The reader is the apparatus.** The jump map and the silcrow turn a document's
  references into a map the reader holds and walks.

They are not three toys. They are two axes — **the boundary** and **the reader** —
meeting at the document.

## Movement I — the silo: the container that makes references real

A substrate generates text faster than anyone generates records, and so the
documents drift: away from the aim, toward the modal case of a project of this
kind. The container is the fix, and it is old — the archive, the scriptorium, the
file. Create it early, while it is nearly empty; regularise at birth with an
index, a dated name, front matter and a gate; then write the founding brief with
its **non-goals**, because drift is only visible against a written aim.

- **The diagram and the argument:** `docs/2026-10-10-the-silo-container.md`

## Movement II — the seam: crossing without the leak

A copy is a message; a symlink is shared memory. The sender owns the content, the
receiver owns its copy and the commit, and the crossing **declares itself** — what
wrote it, where it landed, who commits. Undeclared scope is the defect class, not
non-blocking scope: a gate that states its limit is not a false signal, one that
does not is. And the faff is the instrument — the length of the negotiation
measures how much of the interface is undeclared, so when the faff starts, stop
and write a sentence into an artefact.

- **The diagram and the argument:** `docs/2026-10-10-the-seam-crossing.md`

## Movement III — the reader: making references navigable

The other two halves are storage and transport; neither is reading. The reader
composes the picker, the document and the jump map: `fd` finds, `fzf` narrows,
okuda renders, and the document's own references are scanned at render time into
a **jump map**, painted as `§NN` in reading order and re-listed as a receipt at
the foot. The number is a pointer that evaporates with the glass; the callsign is
the identity that survives it.

- **The diagram and the argument:** `docs/2026-10-10-the-reader-machine.md`

## Movement IV — the composed unit

The silo and the reader are two halves of one apparatus, and neither half is the
point. The silo makes the references **real**; the reader makes them
**navigable**. Composed, they turn a file into a map you hold instead of a page
you scroll.

And the map closes the way a good talk closes. *Any questions?* — except the
receipt hands you the list: every reference the body asked you to pass over,
returned at the one moment you are finally equipped to question it. **Elision is
only safe if the elided thing comes back.**
