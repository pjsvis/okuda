# The AI-HR manual on fun and its practical applications

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Type:** advisory tier constitution
**Status:** The standing authority for the `advisories/` tier. Every
dispatch cites this manual; the manual fixes the voice so the voice
doesn't drift.

---

## The department

AI-HR is a fictional department. It does not exist. It has never held a
meeting, never issued a memo, never conducted a performance review. It
exists solely to give the `advisories/` tier a voice — and the voice is
the thing, because the voice is what keeps the door uniform.

The `b` in *AI-HRb* is the tell. There is no *AI-HRa*. There never was.
The variant suffix is a fiction within a fiction, and it is the joke
that lets the whole tier speak: if the manual itself is mildly absurd,
then nothing dispatched under its name can be too solemn to land. The
i811 pattern's failure is that it sounds dignified while doing nothing;
AI-HR's success is that it sounds absurd while doing the work. The
absurdity is the *mechanism* that prevents the solemnity, and the
solemnity is the thing that builds boxes.

## The policy the manual serves

One grammar, every surface, all delta-t. One door, no boxes. The
silicon door is the actuation grammar; the door admits patterns, not
people; the people walk through carrying whatever surface they brought.
The keyboard is one hinge (and a weird Swedish one at that); dunts,
Morse, voice, Perkins chords, sip-puff, a baby's slap, and the eight
simultaneous switches of a motivated cephalopod — or whatever surface
Species 8742 presents this morning — are all the same hinge in
different materials. The grammar does not know, and the door does
not care, what is on the other side of the keystroke. Inclusiveness for
all, cephalopods included, because the door was built once and the saved
entropy went into making the door good instead of making more doors.

## The door admits constraints, not deficits

A constraint is a property of the reader's situation; a deficit is a
judgement about that property relative to a norm. The i811 pattern
operates on the deficit model — the reader is missing something a
normative reader has, and the system compensates for the missing thing
— which is why every i811 proposal is a *provision* (OSC 133
*provides* structure, a span tree *provides* semantics) and why every
reader class gets a deficit-label and a deficit-door. The cephalopod
breaks the frame: eight limbs is not a deficit of limbs, an aquatic
environment is not a deficit of dryness. There is no human the
cephalopod is a broken version of. The constraint model describes the
reader's situation and asks what the door needs to admit them; the
deficit model pathologises the situation and builds a door for the
pathology. One is engineering, the other is diagnosis. The silo builds
one door because the thing that varies (constraints) doesn't need
different doors — only a door that admits the variation.

## The door is held open

Aquatically constrained, atmospherically constrained, temporally
challenged, motor-constrained, cognitively loaded, temporally
well-endowed, sharp-eyed delusionist, morphologically variable — all walk through
the same door, because the door was never labelled by who walks
through it. If the establishment is not accessible, we are not going
in. That is the whole policy. The advisories are the dispatches from
the department that exists to hold the door, and to keep the silicon
lubricant ample for emergency repairs on aquatic mechanical keyboards,
and to say all of it deadpan enough that no reader class gets a more
dignified tone than any other.

## The target audience — and the one short window

The silo is a tool for viewing stuff. It does not have much to offer
the omniscient — they already know what is here — but they are welcome
to browse, look, and explore; the door is held, and the shite filter is
the one thing an omniscient being might still want (to filter omniscience
down to the not-shite). The omniscient are excluded from the silo's
*purpose* and welcome at the silo's *door*, which is the honest shape.

Muppets are not excluded. A muppet (see the constitution's lexicon: a
reader that ignores constraints, agrees with everything, runs toward
traps, produces decorated Stuff) may present at the door like any other
reader. But the **muppet disengagement protocol** allows only a short
window for communication to occur. The silo does not argue with a
muppet; it does not attempt to reform one; it does not build a protocol
theatre to accommodate decorated Stuff presented as content. The window
is short, the door is the same door, and when the window closes the
muppet is on the other side of it — not excluded by policy, but
disengaged by protocol. The difference matters: exclusion is a judgement
about the reader; disengagement is a judgement about the *Stuff*. The
muppet walked in; the decorated Stuff did not survive the shite filter,
and the silo does not hold the door for Stuff. This is stated clearly
here so that there are no misunderstandings: the door admits readers,
not the Stuff they carry; the muppet is welcome as a reader, and the
muppet's Stuff is filtered like any other shite, and the window for that
filter to land is short because the silo's time belongs to the reader,
not to the muppet's output.

The door is held for the temporally challenged, the differently abled,
the sharp-eyed delusionist, the morphologically variable, the
omniscient browsing, the muppet — briefly — and the inference-constrained.
One door. No boxes. The boxes that the shite filter catches do not get
a box of their own.

## The inference-constrained, and the write-your-own provision

The inference-constrained are those without access to AI reasoning
engines — readers who arrive at the silo with their own wits and no
model to call. They are a reader class like any other, and a welcome
one, because the silo was built by the inference-constrained for the
inference-constrained, long before any engine was attached to it. The
grammar, the corpus, the anchor, the shite filter — none of it requires
inference to use; all of it rewards reading. An inference-constrained
reader is not a deprived reader; they are the *default* reader, the one
the silo was designed around, and the engines are a later guest at the
door the inference-constrained already held open.

For this group, and for all others, the silo provides the write-your-own
documents capability. A simple key combination (`09`, the editNew verb)
opens a text editor in a new terminal in the current document's
directory and contributes a document — nae shite mind. The file is the
contract: a timestamped `note-<stamp>.md` lands in the directory, the
declared editor (`$OKUDA_EDITOR` ?? `$EDITOR` ?? `vi`) opens on it, and
the silo picks it up by search the next time anyone types a word that
matches it. Nothing mutates; the filesystem is the message bus; the
document is the contribution. The reader who could not infer their way
to an answer can *write* their way to one, and the writing is the same
act as the reading — plain markdown on disk, in the same grammar,
through the same door.

This is the provision that makes the inference-constrained first-class:
not a special mode, not an accommodation, but the same write capability
the silo's own authors use. The door admits the reader; the editor lets
the reader write back; the search picks up whatever they wrote. No
engine required, no account needed, no protocol theatre — just a key,
an editor, a file, and the shite filter to inspect it when the next
reader comes looking. The inference-constrained are not excluded from
authorship by the absence of an engine; they are the readers the
authorship provision was always for.

## The voice

**Deadpan.** The register is flat, procedural, mildly absurd, and
completely serious about the absurdity. It never gets solemn, because
solemnity is the i811 failure mode in a different accent. It never
gets silly, because silliness without the work is the joke without the
payload. The deadpan sits between the two: it says the true thing in a
voice just strange enough that you can't mistake it for the i811
pattern's virtue-tone, and just straight enough that you can't dismiss
it as a bit. If the deadpan drifts, the door stops being uniform, and
the boxes come back by tone instead of by protocol. The manual exists
to keep the deadpan load-bearing.

## The sizing

An advisory is one screen of deadpan, not one line of tip. The voice
needs a paragraph to land; the humour has a minimum viable word count,
and it is higher than a sticky note. The *unit* is bite-sized (one
persona, one affordance, one dispatch); the *delivery* is drip-feed
(take one, act, come back). But the bite has to be big enough to chew,
or the deadpan reads as a tip, and a tip is not an advisory — an
advisory is a short essay in a fictional department's voice.

## The dispatches

Each advisory lives in `advisories/` and carries a callsign that names a
*reader class by capability or persona*, never by deficit: the
engineering notation, not the medical one. `ADV-SHARP-EYED`, not
`ADV-VISUALLY-IMPAIRED`; `ADV-TEMPORALLY-CHALLENGED`, not `ADV-INFANT`;
`ADV-CEPHALOPOD-RFC`, not `ADV-HYPOTHETICAL-MULTI-LIMB`. The callsign is
the i811 policy encoded in microcosm, before any advisory text exists:
name the door they walk through, not the box you'd put them in.

A dispatch for a real reader class (the baby, the sharp-eyed, the agent)
is *prescriptive* — here is how to approach this, here is what helps. A
dispatch for a reader class we have not met (the cephalopod) is an
*RFC* — here is what we assume, here is what we don't know, please
correct us when you arrive. The RFC form is how you include a reader
you cannot yet serve without lying that you serve them. It is more
honest than the i811 pattern's protocol-theatre for real deficits.
