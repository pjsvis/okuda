- **The register** — verdict A, by reframe: pitch-down is a casting
  axis, not an attitude axis. Below base, Moira doesn't get somber —
  her auntie fills in. The narrative base stays plain Moira; the gloom
  is the prose's job. The auntie herself goes to the casting test
  above — the bug (pitch sticks) is the feature (a character voice
  that holds a turn), separated only by intent.

  → **2026-09-13 — the auntie gets a job.** BRF-AUDIO-DISCOVERY's
  pending cue (the recital of a half-typed actuator pair) wants to be
  an *aside*, and the aside is a cast, not a pitch. `[[pbas -10]]` is
  already taken by the document's own dialogue
  (`tmp/aunt-interleave.txt` — the Reverend lives at minus ten), so a
  pitch-shifted cue collides with the text it is meant to sit beside;
  a **separate voice** cannot collide. So **Moir speaks the cue
  channel, Moira narrates the document, and the two never swap** —
  the reservation is the mechanism, not a nicety. That makes the
  names row above load-bearing rather than cosmetic: a channel is
  waiting on her to land.

  → **2026-09-13, later — the job widens to the whole cast principle:
  Moira narrates the document, Moir speaks for okuda.** Not the pending
  cue's voice but *the system's* voice — every cue class, alerts and
  notifications included. The canon already drew the line (ADR-032:
  *doubled = the system talks*); the cast names the speaker on the
  system's side of it. Two consequences worth an ear:
  - **The CSN row above is testing the wrong speaker.** `CSN` is the
    *renderer's* diagnostic (*too wide*, *too narrow*), so a refusal is
    **okuda talking** — Moir's line, husk and all. The current row
    tries to make Moira sound like a system by removing the one thing
    that would have marked it as one. Re-run it against Moir.
  - **The reservation gets harder.** It cannot be enforced by voice
    name, because a document's own character lands in Moir's register
    by pitch alone (this file's own verdict, above). The rule has to
    be stronger: **Moir is never a character in the document.**
    Otherwise `tmp/aunt-recital.txt` — a document whose content *is*
    a husky aunt — eats the system's voice.
  - And the thing to try to break first: **one voice carrying both the
    aside and the alarm.** The aside is subordinate, the alarm must be
    noticed. The answer is that the **earcon** carries noticeability
    (it has no voice at all) and urgency lives in *when* and *what*,
    never in *how loud* — but that is the assumption, so dogfood it.
